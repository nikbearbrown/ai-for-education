# AI jobs research

What do employers ask of new graduates? How do those requirements relate to what universities teach? How does access to AI change what people do and learn once they get to work? These are related questions, but they need different evidence.

| Project | What it examines |
| --- | --- |
| [AI Jobs](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/ai-jobs) | Collects postings from public employer boards in a common format. The initial focus is roles relevant to Northeastern’s Software Engineering and Information Systems graduates. |
| [The Junior’s Curve](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/the-juniors-curve) | Tracks what employers ask of entry-level STEM workers. A separate proposed study examines how coding agents affect the work and learning of first-year developers. |
| [Irreducibly Human Work Lexicon](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/irreducibly-human-work-lexicon) | Builds an auditable vocabulary for matching skills mentioned in curricula and job postings. |
| [A New Shape for Work](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/a-new-shape-for-work) | Examines what can be learned from occupational task descriptions, job ads, and published observations of AI use without treating them as the same measure. |
| [Research Jobs](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/research-jobs) | Documents the sources behind a directory of research labs and organizations, including small groups that can be hard for students to find. |

A job posting is evidence of an advertised role, not a hire, a measure of day-to-day work, or proof that AI changed a job. A map entry is a lead, not a confirmed vacancy.

[Browse the expanded paper and research repository index](papers.md). [See publication and evidence status](../docs/research.md) for AI readiness, classroom and project evidence, and related research. Entries distinguish preprints, working drafts, methods, and tools.
